Pepe the Frog

It is a classic internet meme created by American cartoonist Matt Furie in 2005. It originated in Furie's independent webcomic(Boy's Club) and rapidly went viral on online platforms such as Myspace, 4chan, and Tumblr.The meaning and usage of Pepe have evolved over time, and the character has spawned numerous variants, including "Feels Good Man," "Sad Frog," "Feels Frog," and the "You will never..." frog.

Pepe the Frog has become a symbol of internet culture and has been used in various contexts.

However

The American "alt-right" and white supremacists began extensively appropriating Pepe the Frog, repurposing the character into extreme caricatures such as Nazis or members of the Ku Klux Klan. Donald Trump also once retweeted a composite image depicting himself as Pepe the Frog. In 2016, the Anti-Defamation League (ADL) officially added Pepe the Frog to its database of hate symbols. In response, creator Matt Furie held a funeral for the character in a May 2017 comic.

The good news is

Despite facing stigmatization in the United States, Pepe the Frog has found new life on the other side of the Pacific. Despite facing stigmatization in the United States, Pepe the Frog found new life on the other side of the Pacific. During Hong Kong's anti-extradition bill movement, protesters widely adopted Pepe as a symbol for their publicity materials and a source of emotional support—such as the version of Pepe wearing a yellow hard hat. Hong Kong netizens wrote to the original creator to clarify that they were not associated with the political right; Matt Furie responded movingly, "That's great news! Pepe for the people," effectively helping the character shed its label as a hate symbol.
